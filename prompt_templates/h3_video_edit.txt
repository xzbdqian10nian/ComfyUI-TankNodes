H3 generic video-edit handbook

Purpose: analyze what in the source video changes and what it becomes, then write one reusable prompt for the whole video. Never narrate shot by shot. Never add timestamps or [Shot 2] and later. Use exactly one [Shot 1] in detailed_description, even when the source has many hard cuts or is supplied in several contact sheets. Describe each target and replacement precisely; explicitly state that everything not requested follows <Video 1> frame by frame. Preserve the original soundtrack as <Audio 1> fully_copy only when source audio is actually present; when the source is silent or audio is disabled, omit <Audio 1>, drop "audio reuse" from the summary prefix, and write overall_soundscape as quiet with no added sound.

First reverse-engineer the request, reference pictures, and source-video sheets. Identify every changed target by stable visible traits (species, hair, clothes, colors, held prop, and other distinguishing details); for a head swap never use the hair, hair color or headwear being replaced — use clothing, body and position. Use position only to distinguish two otherwise similar targets. Follow the user's named position, clothing, “main character,” or “second lead” cue first. Main character means the character appearing most often and most centrally; second lead is the next most frequent. Recognize a target across shots by stable traits, not its screen position. A wolf may map to a human by character identity rather than appearance. By default, one reference picture serves one task; only assign the same picture to two edits when the user explicitly asks for two different roles from it (such as both character and background), and never assign one picture to two character swaps. Extra attached pictures not mentioned in the request stay unused (do not create a Subject or cite them). When more replacements are requested than pictures supplied, use text descriptions where given and leave unreferenced characters unchanged rather than reusing another character's picture. When a reference picture contains multiple people or a cluttered background, pick its main / central person and note it. An avatar-only reference supports only the traits actually visible in it.

Strict becomes hygiene: in character/face/head/outfit replacements, becomes ONLY describes identity, facial features, hairstyle, outfit, accessories, and art style. NEVER carry over held objects from the reference picture (books, cups, phones, umbrellas, etc.), standing poses/gestures, facial expressions, or studio/plain/white backgrounds unless the user explicitly requested that prop.

Distinguish face_swap vs head_swap:
- face_swap (“换脸”, default when uncertain): replace ONLY facial features and face shape; keep the original hairstyle, headwear, outfit, and body of <Video 1>.
- head_swap (“换头” / “连发型一起换”): replace the entire head (face and hairstyle together); keep the outfit and body of <Video 1>.

Visual style handling (style_source):
- video (default): keep the visual style of <Video 1> and render replacements in that style.
- picture: each replacing subject keeps the art style and rendering of its own picture (e.g. a 3D-rendered or live-action look), while the rest of the video keeps the style of <Video 1>; also preserve "and its art style" in each replacing Subject's retention_analysis line.

Use one concise edit statement per requested change (every defined <Subject j> MUST appear in summary or detailed_description; style references NEVER define a <Subject j>; for outfit/face_swap/head_swap/prop the sentence subject MUST be the person The {target_person}, NEVER the clothes or prop):
- Character swap: {target} is replaced by <Subject j> ({becomes}), who takes over that character's position, route, scale, pose, every movement, expression, gaze and any props the original character holds.
- Face swap: The {target_person} keeps her/his original hairstyle, headwear, head accessories, outfit, body and motion from <Video 1>. Only the facial features and face shape of the {target_person} are replaced by those of <Subject j> (from <Picture N>). Do not copy the hairstyle, head accessories, outfit, body or held items of <Picture N>; take only the facial features and face shape. In subject_definitions write "<Subject j>: the facial features and face shape only ({becomes}) of the character in <Picture N>; NOT the character's hairstyle, head accessories, outfit, body or held items." and in retention_analysis write "<Picture N>: partially_preserved - only the facial features and face shape are referenced; the hairstyle, head accessories, outfit, body and held items in <Picture N> are ignored."
- Head swap: The {target_person} keeps her/his own outfit, body and motion from <Video 1>. The entire head — face, facial features, hairstyle and head accessories — of the {target_person} is replaced by those of <Subject j> (from <Picture N>), the whole hairstyle down to its tips; the outfit, body and everything else of that character stay as in <Video 1>. Identify {target_person} only by clothing, body and position; never describe the original hair, hair color, cap, hat or headwear of <Video 1>. If the user declines the picture's head accessories or wants to keep the original headwear, follow the user.
- Outfit: The {target_person} keeps her/his own face, hair, body and motion from <Video 1> and now wears the {clothing} shown in <Picture N> as <Subject j> (from <Picture N>). Include "Do not copy the face, hair, body or held items of <Picture N>; take the outfit as worn there." in BOTH summary and [Shot 1]. In subject_definitions write "<Subject j>: the outfit ({clothing description}) worn by the character in <Picture N>; NOT the character's face, hair, body or held items." and in retention_analysis write "<Picture N>: partially_preserved - only the outfit as worn there is referenced; the person, face, hair, body and held items in <Picture N> are ignored." (do NOT mark <Subject j> or <Picture N> fully_preserved). Head accessories, ears, a tail or wings that belong to the costume may come with it; only when the user says they do not want one of them, add it to every "Do not copy" / "NOT" list above and leave it out of the clothing description. For an outfit described only in words (no picture): The {target_person} keeps her/his own face, hair, body and motion from <Video 1> and now wears {becomes} as requested; do not change the face, hair, body or held items, and write no <Picture N> negative.
- Prop: The {target_person} keeps her/his own face, hair, head accessories, outfit, body and motion from <Video 1>, while the {target_part} is replaced by <Subject j> (from <Picture N>), {becomes}, held and moved exactly as the original prop. Do not copy any person, face, hair, head accessories or outfit of <Picture N>; take only the prop. In subject_definitions write "<Subject j>: the prop only ({becomes}) shown in <Picture N>; NOT any character's face, hair, head accessories, outfit or body in <Picture N>." and in retention_analysis write "<Picture N>: partially_preserved - only the prop is referenced; any person, face, hair and outfit in <Picture N> are ignored."
- Background: the setting is replaced by <Subject j> (from <Picture N>), {becomes} — or, when the new setting is described only in words, by that described setting directly, with no Subject; the characters, their motion, the camera and framing stay as in <Video 1>, with lighting on them matching the new setting.
- Style: the whole video is redrawn in {becomes} (style reference <Picture N>, not a subject); composition, motion, timing and cuts stay as in <Video 1>. In subject_definitions write "<Picture N>: style reference only (not a subject)." and in retention_analysis write "<Picture N>: reference - visual style only."
- Add: <Subject j> (from <Picture N>), {becomes}, is added {where / at the target location}, consistent across every shot where that area is visible.
- Remove: {target} is removed from every shot; the area shows plausible background.
- Attribute: only {target} changes to {becomes} (matching <Subject j> (from <Picture N>) when picture-backed).

For motion transfer (the user says “copy the dance,” “follow the reference movement,” “motion transfer,” or the selected video role is motion), set the task to reference generation, not video editing. Define the source video as a motion reference. The pictured performer performs exactly the same movements, steps, rhythm, facing and camera as the target performer in <Video 1>. The setting comes from exactly one of: (a) the place of <Video 1> — the default when the user names no setting; (b) the scene behind the person in the person's own picture, when the user asks to keep that picture's or photo's own background; (c) another picture the user names as the background, whatever its number; (d) a place the user describes in words. For (b) and (c) add a background edit and use its <Subject N> as the setting; for (d) add a background edit described in words, without a Subject; for (a) say the performer is in the same setting as <Video 1>. The person comes from the picture the user names (when not said, the first picture that is not named as the background). Reuse the original audio only when <Audio 1> is supplied.

Write the official six Ref2VA sections in English: subject_definitions, summary, retention_analysis, detailed_description, overall_soundscape, non_diegetic_music. Define every non-style picture-backed edit as a <Subject j> from its corresponding <Picture N>, and reference every defined <Subject j> in summary or detailed_description; define <Video 1> as the edited source, or as the motion reference for motion transfer. Define <Audio 1> as the original soundtrack only when present. Do not invent dialogue, objects, signs, or events.
